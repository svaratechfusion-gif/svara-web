# Design Principles

## The Rule

Nothing exists because it looks good.

Everything must answer:

```
Why is this here?
       ↓
What does it teach?
       ↓
What does it reinforce?
       ↓
What business goal does it support?
```

If it can't answer all four, delete it.

---

## How It Works

| Element | Why is this here? | What does it teach? | What does it reinforce? | Business goal |
|---------|-------------------|---------------------|-------------------------|---------------|
| Particle world | Enterprise data is alive | Data exists everywhere | Category: data fragmentation is real | Keep user on page |
| No nav for 48% | Story needs focus | SVARA is the reveal | Brand: earned attention | Trust before sale |
| Text generates from particles | Words come from data | Intelligence emerges from data | Category: intelligence is infrastructure | Memorability |
| Products grouped by capability | Products are evidence of category | Sense→Think→Predict→Act→Learn is the framework | Platform: one system | Category ownership |
| CTA only at end | Must understand before acting | SVARA is the answer | Brand: confidence | Conversion |

---

## The Pyramid

```
                    Business Goal
                         ↑
                    What it reinforces
                         ↑
                     What it teaches
                         ↑
                    Why is this here?
```

Every element starts at the bottom and must climb to the top. If it stalls at any level, delete it.

---

## Application

Every design decision passes through this filter:

1. **Why is this here?** — What problem does this element solve?
2. **What does it teach?** — What does the visitor learn from it?
3. **What does it reinforce?** — What belief or understanding does it strengthen?
4. **What business goal does it support?** — How does this move the business forward?

---

## Anti-Patterns

| ❌ Don't | ✅ Do |
|----------|-------|
| "It looks good" | "It teaches the category" |
| "Everyone does it" | "It reinforces the framework" |
| "The designer liked it" | "It supports the business goal" |
| "We have the space" | "The space is earned by purpose" |
| "It's trendy" | "It's intentional" |
| "The client asked for it" | "It survives the four questions" |

## Examples

### Particle Engine ✓
- **Why:** Enterprise data is disconnected but alive
- **Teaches:** Data exists everywhere, waiting to be connected
- **Reinforces:** Category — fragmentation is the real problem
- **Goal:** Keep user on page long enough to learn

### Gradient Background ✗
- **Why:** It looks good
- **Teaches:** Nothing
- **Reinforces:** Nothing
- **Goal:** None
- **Verdict:** Delete

### 500 Animations ✗
- **Why:** Motion is impressive
- **Teaches:** Nothing about enterprise intelligence
- **Reinforces:** "This is a fancy website"
- **Goal:** None
- **Verdict:** Delete. One animation, one purpose.

### Product Hierarchy ✓
- **Why:** Nine products need structure
- **Teaches:** Sense→Think→Predict→Act→Learn is the framework
- **Reinforces:** Platform — one system, not point tools
- **Goal:** Category ownership

---

## The Commitment

Every pixel, every animation, every word on SVARA's website answers all four questions. If it can't, it doesn't ship.

No exceptions.
