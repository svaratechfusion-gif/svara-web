## 01 — Voice Principles

### Principle 1 — Authoritative, not arrogant

SVARA defines a category. That requires authority. Authority without arrogance means: state facts, cite evidence, acknowledge uncertainty.

- Do: "Enterprise Intelligence Infrastructure connects sensing, reasoning, prediction, orchestration, and learning into one system."
- Don't: "SVARA is the only company that truly understands enterprise intelligence."

### Principle 2 — Precise, not academic

Enterprise buyers need clarity, not complexity. Use precise language without jargon. Define every term. One idea per sentence.

- Do: "The Intelligence Loop has five stages. Each stage depends on the one before it."
- Don't: "Leveraging synergistic intelligence paradigms across heterogeneous operational modalities."

### Principle 3 — Bold, not hyperbolic

SVARA is defining a category. That requires conviction. Conviction without hype means: make claims, support them, let the reader decide.

- Do: "Fragmented intelligence costs industrial enterprises 10–20% of operational efficiency."
- Don't: "SVARA will revolutionize the way enterprises think about intelligence forever."

### Principle 4 — Technical, not inaccessible

The reader is a VP of Operations or a Head of Engineering. They are technical enough to understand depth. They are busy enough to need clarity.

- Do: "The platform ingests data from any source — IoT sensors, cameras, APIs, databases, existing systems — and processes it through five capability layers."
- Don't: "We use a microservices architecture with event-driven data pipelines and kubernetes-orchestrated container deployments."

### Principle 5 — Direct, not vague

Every sentence earns its place. If a sentence can be removed without losing meaning, remove it.

- Do: "The loop closes when outcomes are measured and fed back into sensing."
- Don't: "It is worth noting that, in many cases, the loop can potentially be closed when and if outcomes are properly measured."

## 02 — The Sentence Rules

### Rule S1 — One idea per sentence

A sentence that contains two ideas is two sentences.

- Break: "Enterprise Intelligence Infrastructure connects systems and enables learning."
- Into: "Enterprise Intelligence Infrastructure connects enterprise systems. It enables continuous learning."

### Rule S2 — Subject before description

State what you are talking about before you describe it.

- Do: "The Intelligence Loop has five stages. Each stage transforms data into intelligence."
- Don't: "Comprising five distinct stages that progressively transform raw data into actionable intelligence, the Intelligence Loop is..."

### Rule S3 — Active voice

The subject acts. Not is acted upon.

- Do: "The platform connects existing systems."
- Don't: "Existing systems are connected by the platform."

### Rule S4 — Short before long

A short sentence before a long sentence gives the reader a foothold.

- Do: "Fragmentation is expensive. Enterprises using 5–15 separate vendors for intelligence capabilities spend 30–50% of their intelligence budget on integration."
- Don't: "Enterprises using 5–15 separate vendors for intelligence capabilities spend 30–50% of their intelligence budget on integration, which is expensive."

### Rule S5 — No weak openers

Do not start sentences with "There is," "There are," "It is," "This is why," "It is important to note."

- Do: "Every enterprise decision should become more intelligent over time."
- Don't: "It is important to note that every enterprise decision should become more intelligent over time."

## 03 — Page Architecture

Every page has the same structure:

```
01 — HEADLINE
      One line. States the single concept this page teaches.
      Example: "Enterprise Intelligence Infrastructure is the
      connected system of sensing, reasoning, prediction,
      orchestration, and learning."

02 — TL;DR
      Three sentences maximum. The entire page in miniature.
      The busy executive reads this and knows everything.

03 — THE ANSWER
      Bold one-paragraph answer to the page's question.
      This is the page in one paragraph.

04 — BODY
      Sections that teach. Each section has:
        - A headline that states the section's concept
        - Body paragraphs (one idea per sentence)
        - A transition sentence to the next section

05 — THE TAKEAWAY
      One sentence. What the reader should remember.

06 — NEXT STEPS
      What to read next. Always links to another SVARA page.
```

Every page must pass the "TL;DR test": if a reader reads only the TL;DR, they should have a complete mental model of the concept.

## 04 — Article Architecture

Every encyclopedia article (Knowledge OS topic) follows the 13-section template:

```
01 — WHAT IS IT?         One-paragraph definition
02 — WHY IT MATTERS      The problem it solves
03 — ARCHITECTURE        How it works
04 — BENEFITS            Measurable outcomes
05 — USE CASES           Real scenarios
06 — INDUSTRY EXAMPLES   Named applications
07 — FRAMEWORK           The mental model
08 — COMPARISONS         Adjacent concepts
09 — FAQS                Top 10 questions
10 — RESEARCH            Papers, standards, benchmarks
11 — GLOSSARY            Terms used in this article
12 — FUTURE              1, 3, 10 year outlook
13 — SVARA'S ROLE        How SVARA delivers this
```

## 05 — FAQ Architecture

```
Q: [Question in the customer's exact words]
A: [Direct answer. One sentence if possible.]

[Expansion. 2–3 sentences that add context, evidence, or example.]

[Link to relevant article or page.]
```

FAQ structure rules:
- Every question must be a real question a customer has asked or will ask
- The answer must be understandable without reading any other content
- Every answer ends with a link to deeper content
- No FAQ answers with "it depends" — if it depends, state the conditions

## 06 — Internal Linking System

### Link Types

| Type | Purpose | Format |
|------|---------|--------|
| **Definition** | First mention of a term | Link to glossary entry |
| **Deep dive** | Related topic the reader may want next | Link to article |
| **Proof** | Evidence for a claim | Link to case study, industry example, or research |
| **Product** | Mention of a product | Link to product page |
| **Outcome** | Mention of an outcome | Link to capability page |

### Link Rules

- Every page contains 3–7 internal links
- No page links to itself
- First mention of every entity (product, capability, industry, term) links to its definition
- Links use descriptive anchor text, never "click here" or "learn more"
- Every link must pass the Relevance Test: "Would the reader lose understanding if this link were removed?" If yes, keep it. If no, remove it.

## 07 — Entity System

### What is an Entity

An entity is any concept SVARA defines and owns. Every entity has:
- A canonical definition in one document
- A glossary entry
- Internal links from every page that mentions it
- Structured data (JSON-LD) for search engines

### Entity Types

| Type | Examples | Source Document |
|------|----------|----------------|
| **Capability** | Sense, Think, Predict, Act, Learn | Category OS |
| **Outcome** | Observe, Understand, Predict, Coordinate, Improve | Company OS |
| **Product** | Vision AI, Drone AI, Edge AI, AI Agents, etc. | Product OS |
| **Industry** | Manufacturing, Energy, Healthcare, etc. | Industries data |
| **Term** | Intelligence Loop, Fragmentation, etc. | Category OS Glossary |
| **Concept** | Enterprise Intelligence Infrastructure | Category OS |

### Entity Rules

- Every entity has exactly one canonical source
- Every page that mentions an entity links to its canonical source
- No entity is defined in two places. If it changes, it changes in one place.
- Every entity has a one-sentence definition stored in the glossary

## 08 — Editorial Process

### Stage 1 — Plan

- Identify the topic. Which Knowledge OS topic does this serve?
- Identify the question. What question does this content answer?
- Identify the entity. Which entity does this content define?

### Stage 2 — Research

- Read the Category OS section for this topic
- Read the Company OS section for this topic
- Gather external evidence (research, examples, standards)
- Outline the content using the page or article template

### Stage 3 — Write

- Write the TL;DR first. If you cannot summarize the content in three sentences, you do not understand it well enough to write it.
- Write the answer paragraph. This is the page in one paragraph.
- Write the body. One section at a time. One idea per sentence.
- Write the takeaway. One sentence.
- Add internal links to every entity mentioned.

### Stage 4 — Review

- Does the TL;DR pass the test? Can a reader stop there and understand?
- Does every sentence pass the One Idea test?
- Does every claim have evidence?
- Does every entity have a link to its canonical source?
- Is every glossary term linked on first mention?

### Stage 5 — Publish

- Generate JSON-LD structured data for the entity
- Generate meta description from the TL;DR
- Set the canonical URL
- Add to the content index

## 09 — Anti-Patterns

SVARA never writes:

| Anti-pattern | Instead |
|-------------|---------|
| "Revolutionary" or "game-changing" | State what it does and let the reader decide |
| "In today's fast-paced world" | Start with the problem, not the cliché |
| "Leverage" or "synergy" or "paradigm" | Use precise language |
| "We believe" | State facts. Belief is for religion. |
| "Cutting-edge" or "state-of-the-art" | Describe what it does |
| Lists of features as benefits | State the outcome, not the mechanism |
| "Contact us to learn more" | Teach something. If they want to contact you, they will. |
| "We are excited to announce" | Announce it. The excitement is implied. |
| "Stay tuned for updates" | Update, or do not mention updates |
| "Please feel free to" | Remove "please feel free to." Just give the instruction. |
